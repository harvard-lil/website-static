GENERATIONAL DATA INTERVIEWS

14 DESIGNS FOR DIGITAL PRESERVATION IN 2025

WE ASKED A SINGLE QUESTION:

If you were given unlimited funding to design a system for storing and preserving digital information for at least a century, what would you do?

Interviews

Katie Mackinnon
Lori Emerson
Che-Wei Wang & Taylor Levy
Michelle Lee
Ian Milligan
Trevor Owens

Last year, the Library Innovation Lab published Century-Scale Storage, a sprawling multinarrative exhaustive look into the current state of long term digital storage and preservation.

To broaden this discussion and capture a slice of this particular moment, we interviewed 14 scholars, archivists, designers, business leaders, and engineers about their pie in the sky ideas, their dreams, their fears, and the lessons they have drawn from their years of practice.

We asked each of them a single question: If you were given unlimited funding to design a system for storing and preserving digital information for at least a century, what would you do?

The responses were as diverse as the people we interviewed—from the practical to the philosophical, from the technical to the social, from the individual to the institutional.

Interviewer

MAXWELL NEELY-COHEN

Max is a novelist, essayist, and editor who loves figuring out how to write about emerging fields, tools, and practices. Outside of his literary ventures, his work with design, code, and performance has been acclaimed by The New York Times, Frieze, and The Financial Times. He is obsessed with libraries and archives.
